# Integrated concepts and experiments

## What would show a benefit from targeted recurrence?

The team proposed four mechanisms: an output-fed decoder block (A), refinement of a fixed visual crop (B), constraint-directed source acquisition (C), and bounded program repair (D). The leading document hypothesis couples B and C: competing tables identify a cell worth rereading, then a reader uses that cell's actual pixels. The baseline pairs the same exact solver with the strongest frozen simple reader. The three amended clauses below determine which extra computation and which new evidence belong to the treatment.

A compares stock K=0, a same-input duplicate K=0 with two physical calls, and the first output-fed treatment K=1. Count K per selected position and physical calls separately: R=1+K on a literal arm. At five selected positions, one literal extra pass yields five extra evaluations. Preserve each alternative's cache and guard state. If two first tokens agree but a cache scalar changes from 0 to 2, next scores (1-c,c) predict 0 versus 1; use linked continuations as well as fixed-prefix comparisons. TOKEN, native phase validity and the selected-state obligation in Paper II section 2 are amended by the shared clauses below.

B fixes crop bytes and solves its declared visual optimization problem. In the Paper I scalar witness the fixed point is (b-0.05)/1.25 and decision threshold 0.5. At b=0.675 the limit is exactly the boundary; extra iterations starting below it cannot give a strict crossing. For a noiseless 0.9 feature, anchor -0.2 and weight lambda, solving (0.9-0.2 lambda)/(1+lambda)<=0.5 gives lambda>=4/7. These limits in Gemini's advisory demonstrate why better optimization need not improve a reading. A3 likewise requires a contraction on its specified domain and a sufficiently bounded readout gap; those conditions have not been observed for native B11.

C retains the C2 two-clock comparison. Query selection/acquisition advances the external history; latent iterations refine the same bytes. Freeze a matched plan, equal allowed field boxes, costs and source bytes, and compare simple vs recurrent reader at fixed planning. Then score the separately adaptive runs using their realized plans. The history-conditioned law and finite stop in C1 replace the old noisy selection rule in Paper II section 4. An exact constraint solver cannot identify truth outside its candidate set. A calibrated model can still accept an incorrect printed value. D remains the predecessor's bounded, verifier-dependent proposal. A protected test suite needs sound labels; otherwise a correct candidate can be ruled out.

For a future locked cohort define Y_ij=1 if field i exactly matches adjudicated printed truth under arm j. Include every scheduled target once (N>0); a failure, timeout or abstention is zero. Paired exact accuracy change is (recoveries-regressions)/N. Separately report coverage and wrong/posted, undefined when none posted, per-family grouping, acquisition counts and measured time/cost at a prespecified cap. Predeclare family sampling, calibration, uncertainty, risk and success thresholds before access. A nonpositive paired gain against the fixed-plan simple reader falsifies a positive point-gain claim on that cohort. Planning and reader interaction can reverse the adaptive comparison. Numbers from synthetic clause checks give no estimate of either gain.

The remaining design, four concept baselines, D1 verifier proof and cost/efficacy criteria are retained in full from Edition 02. A minimal immediate discriminator is to compare an informative channel to a field with the same crisp partition and identical noisy likelihoods across candidates. C1 selects only the informative action. Assessing improvement on actual source documents remains an open experiment.
