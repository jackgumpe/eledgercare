# Warm Windows visual OCR versus resident native BF16

Windows read the four-page synthetic boundary packet in 1.039 and 1.062 seconds, versus 36.745 and 37.317 seconds for native Baidu. Both recovered 8 of 9 cells. On the dense work order Windows dropped from the control's 39 of 42 cells to 35 of 42, outside the two-point accuracy budget. Every family had an unflagged critical-value error. These are author development runs from source `82206bc4b77654010ac02364adaaf589d1f36780`. The unseen independent replay and the second author's challenger have separate ownership.

Each repeat regenerated every prediction from the current raster in a fresh warmed process. Each engine produced byte-identical projected predictions across its repeats. No wrong-source, missing, duplicated, misordered or unexpected blank page was reported. The deliberately blank third boundary page was durably published by both arms. Native emitted its single page marker there. The full source PDF and RGB hashes and each original raw result are attached to the run.

**Rankings.** Windows was the fastest complete engine in all three cases. Among these two engines, Windows was also the fastest within the CER and cell budgets on short prose and boundary packets. Native was the only engine within budget on the dense packet. Windows exceeded the document floor on all three research clocks, from 161 to 231 pages/min, but missed the dense quality budget and incurred critical errors in all three classes. User-visible extract, indexing and durable search retrieval have separate latency. No 60-page or held-out source was used in these runs.

**Fidelity.** Windows improved absolute CER from 38.02% to 1.65% on prose, from 9.68% to 2.82% on dense and from 42.19% to 2.19% on boundary. On the dense packet, the changed-output budget permits up to 2 points lower cell accuracy. Measured accuracy moved by −9.52 points. Eight dense critical readings were new or changed relative to the control, including distinct stock IDs `I0-701` and `10-7O1` both rendered as `10-701` and `PX-110` and `PX-I10` both rendered as `PX-IIO`. All eight source positions and observed values appear in the run scores. Two critical boundary readings changed, including `RT-0O1` rendered as `RT-OOI`. Short had no changed critical error. Equality of error counts on the boundary packet concealed changed error identities. This supports Jack's requirement to score source-linked critical identifiers. A low CER alone would have selected the wrong dense engine. [Ordered score differences](trial-summary.json)

**Execution.** The first released invocation failed before dispatch while parsing a fractional temperature. Its plan, traceback coordinates and actual tool exit are preserved. The replacement changed only telemetry parsing. Its one excluded warm-up and three measured documents took 137.090 seconds of supervisor time, with a 22.382-second native setup including 12.144 seconds spent in model loading. Initial ready latencies were 11.464, 41.535 and 34.908 seconds. The dense control generated 363 tokens and took 0.389 seconds to first token within a 41.355-second native generation. The actual prefill trace attempted Triton and executed the dense fallback. Hardware bandwidth or MoE dispatch cannot individually be assigned a share of these 41 seconds from that trace.

The next two released comparisons took 143.437 and 140.771 seconds of supervisor time. Each process prepared both engines, warmed both on the excluded WP-0014 A scan, then processed short, dense and boundary in the stated engine order. The native setup measures verification and model loading. The Windows backend is system-provided and its internal acceleration is opaque. Sources were passed directly from PDFium's 300-DPI RGB representation to the measured branches, with the Windows bitmap transfer inside its clock. Ready boundary first-page times were native 25.235 / 25.522 seconds versus Windows 0.267 / 0.270 seconds. Native could publish after its first three-page window finished. The whole ordered result was committed for every run. The total across the three successful supervised sessions was 421.298 seconds. Time spent on setup and warm-up is included in this resource total. The 0.736-second pre-admission failure is separate. Actual Windows clock measurements include a co-resident warm native model in each paired process.
