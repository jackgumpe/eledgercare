## Research-led architecture handoffs

Write each architecture handoff as a research report that another investigator can understand, challenge and reproduce offline. This applies to first proposals, amendments and successor handoffs.

Open with the scientific question, the mechanism under study, its baseline and the evidence that motivates it. Organize the body around the claims whose truth would change the architectural decision. Put each change beside its model, assumptions, derivation or proof, a worked example, a boundary or counterexample, and the observation that could decide it. Define symbols, dimensions, units and index conventions at first use. Choose the mathematical depth the claim needs. An equation count or page count is never a measure of research quality.

Identify what was observed, what follows conditionally, what the examples disprove, and what remains hypothetical. For performance claims, give the comparator, population and unit, metric and denominator, treatment of failures or abstentions, and a falsifier. Give meaningful null or negative results the same visibility as positive results.

Integrate independent criticism where it changes the argument. Attribute the original claim, identify the changed premise and its consequence, and preserve the submitted report as a clearly labeled source when it is part of the handoff. A new reader should be able to reconstruct the operative mathematics from the report itself. Supply the full relevant predecessor, data or small executable example when it materially improves reproducibility.

Conclude with the smallest experiment that discriminates the remaining explanations. Keep source pins, custody, roles, publication and release details in a short closing note or appendix. Bring an operational constraint into the research body when it changes the model, the evidence or the inference. Scientific conclusions and authorization to execute have distinct owners and evidence in the existing workspace agreement.
