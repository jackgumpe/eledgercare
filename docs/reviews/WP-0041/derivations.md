## Greedy scores

For fixed allowed set A and fixed deterministic ordering on ties, write z1 and z2 for the processed scores of a matched decision. With unique L1 winner a, define m_j = z1[a] - z1[j] and delta_i = z2[i] - z1[i]. Then z2[a] - z2[j] = m_j - (delta_j - delta_a). Strictly positive rival gain beyond m_j puts j above a; j can still lose to a third token. Equality activates the tie order if the scores are at the top. The unique winner persists exactly when the difference on the left is positive for every rival. If |delta_i| <= epsilon on A, every relative gain is at most 2 epsilon. Strict lead above 2 epsilon is sufficient; equality permits a tie, and a smaller lead also permits no change. Changed masks and stochastic selection break the stated premises. For the fixed affine head with weight row w_i, raw change equals w_i dot (u2-u1), u_r = RMSNorm(h_r). The pairwise raw gain is bounded in magnitude by ||w_j-w_a|| times ||u2-u1|| by Cauchy-Schwarz. Strict bounds below every respective raw lead protect the raw winner. Nonidentity processors require a separate processed comparison.

For [1.40,1.00] and [1.25,1.30], lead 0.40 loses to relative gain 0.45 by 0.05. Independent truth picks between recovery and regression. ## Mean compute

Let p be the page fraction triggering exactly one field, a the incremental loop state cost averaged over ALL pages, b the remaining loop cost conditional on a trigger, c the corresponding crop cost, all in ms with the same workload. Mean total loop minus crop time is a+p(b-c) after canceling shared first pass, by conditioning on the trigger. Use disjoint cost terms. For a >= 0 and c>b, strict saving iff p>a/(c-b); equality ties. For c<=b no strict saving with a>=0. A threshold >=1 cannot yield strict saving for a valid p<=1. At a=2,b=3,c=11 the break-even is 1/4 and the differences at p=0.1, 0.25, 0.5 are +1.2, 0 and -2 ms/page. A faster field alone can lose overall. Changing batching, memory transfers, prefix rebuild, divergent lengths, hardware or population changes the cost assumptions. Compare actual warm and cold end-to-end times, peak memory, skipped-page overhead, token/reconstruction counts and physical field outcomes before any release decision.
