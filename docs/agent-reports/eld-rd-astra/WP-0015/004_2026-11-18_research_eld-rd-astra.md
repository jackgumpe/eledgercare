## Competing mechanisms and decision

**Windows visual reader.** The independent hypothesis was that recognizing source pixels once and retaining word geometry would remove the long serial decode. It did. In both fresh development orders Windows completed the short scan in 0.281–0.307 seconds, the dense work order in 0.340–0.372 and the four-page boundary packet in 1.039–1.062. The dense cell loss falsified the prediction of a quality-budget win on ruled tables. Four errors appeared at previously correct dense critical positions. Cell counts on the easier development boundary packet matched, yet the unseen H and A packets lost cells. Windows emitted real source-linked word boxes. Its internal compute backend was opaque. Native retained the real model’s token IDs, page markers, stops and geometry. [All individually timed runs, quality counts and source files](../../../research/WP-0015/astra/development-results.md)

**Carbon’s BF16 treatment.** The compound combined packed BF16 mixture-of-experts dispatch, static key/value storage with sparse gathering, GPU logit guards in bursts of eight and disabled per-token console streaming. On dense development data the native/treatment pairs were 36.572 / 31.812 seconds and 40.394 / 38.122 seconds. Each arm recovered 39/42 cells and made four unflagged critical errors out of 24. The treatment was faster on both runs. Its 4.760- and 2.272-second savings straddle the 3.822-second native spread, so the two-order decision rule fails. Full token parity also failed. All four dense predictions scored 48/496 frozen edits and 5/496 title-aware edits, so the compound passed the changed-output quality budget. It was the fastest in-budget observation for this development table. A narrower packed-MoE plus no-streamer ablation took 35.832 seconds against a 44.737-second profiled baseline. That profiled comparison cannot substitute for the two uninstrumented orders. The measured system difference cannot assign the gain to any one intervention inside that treatment. [Carbon’s independently frozen starting position](https://github.com/jackgumpe/eledgercare/blob/8acea1ca42aa23ad48a752c642107b9e9f883f47/docs/research/WP-0015/carbon/position.md) · [Dense AB measurements](https://github.com/jackgumpe/eledgercare/blob/6e859b0dd0e250922cd9d60916a8414d4bd47fa3/docs/coordination/WP-0015/carbon-dense-ab-return.json)

On unseen H the reviewer’s ordinary/compound pairs were **51.214 / 44.566** and **50.982 / 45.393** seconds. The compound saved 6.647 and 5.589 seconds, each above the 0.232-second native spread. This independent speed gain amounted to 5.29–5.39 pages/minute on this four-page packet. Both arms scored 18/20 cells, 178/570 frozen edits, 2/570 title-aware edits and the identical two unflagged critical errors among 19. The compound introduced zero new critical errors. Its boxes moved by one normalized unit in some cases and full token sequences differed. Among all three measured H engines, Windows was fastest complete and Carbon was fastest inside the quality budget. I would carry the compound forward as a changed-output candidate for native acceleration on similar packets. The dense result and untested scale performance limit that recommendation. Carbon had no 60-page timing in this trial. [Reviewer’s independent H comparison](https://github.com/jackgumpe/eledgercare/commit/db4a6df932a74535c9d677c2db561594b4368624)

**Strongest case for a different decision.** Keeping native alone leaves the 60-page research result more than 15 minutes over the two-minute floor, and still leaves 38 critical errors for an operator to find. The Windows result exposes all 60 pages for review in about 16 seconds. A source-linked preview could be valuable even when a critical-value export must wait. Its 15.655-second measured cost leaves roughly 104 seconds of the two-minute floor for automatic routing, verification, fallback and persistence combined. After fixing heading parsing, Windows still made 1,046 fewer character edits on A and avoided all 37 surplus native cells. The unresolved question is whether a selector operating on the scan and reader outputs can catch the newly wrong digits and cells within the remaining time. Charge the cost of selection, all fallback windows and final writes on new matched scans, and score missed critical errors per source position. No such selector or human-review time was measured here. My proposed next comparison would measure that assisted workflow. The case for further BF16 dispatch work is strongest where a second reader changes ambiguous source identifiers. The disposition we coordinated retains native for source-linked review and defers a production architecture choice.
