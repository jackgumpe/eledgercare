[Admission and driver](https://github.com/jackgumpe/eledgercare/tree/eae6b804f2e3220d040d67560d2d82e0be278826/docs/coordination/WP-0039)

My planner reconstructs true points by subtracting signed errors. For each query subset it compares projected finite tuples directly. Within an affine family, two distinct integers disagree on every projected nonzero-slope coordinate and agree on every constant coordinate. Against a finite point, a moving coordinate can match at just one integer parameter, if the slope divides the difference and the root is allowed. The greatest number of votes for one common root gives the most simultaneous matches. Every remaining allowed integer matches zero moving coordinates. This covers all integers, including negative values and arbitrarily large magnitudes. [Projection implementation](https://github.com/jackgumpe/eledgercare/blob/2989d1ad2295089ea325fa0e1d1c392fad2249d3/docs/reviews/WP-0039/planner.py)

I inspected the author’s alternate construction of all distinct nonempty difference supports. For a finite point versus a family it groups allowed equality roots by parameter, then separately includes the generic support with an explicit allowed integer above all roots and exclusions. For the interior of the family, any two distinct allowed integers give the same support. All finite pairs are compared. A pair’s projected distance is exactly the number of queried coordinates in its support. Thus a subset separates the candidates precisely when it meets every support. [Reviewed source](https://github.com/jackgumpe/eledgercare/blob/15bc8cb7f1710b03f2f34de5b9a3b1d8b85a70f8/docs/research/WP-0039/planner.py)

For the post-freeze audit, I recomputed projection collisions using exact equality constraints, and decoded both referenced ledgers for every zero-distance mask on each route. When a nonsingleton projection has no collision, its exact distance is one plus the smallest distance among its single-coordinate deletions. A closest pair loses one disagreement if a differing coordinate is removed, and deleting one coordinate can decrease no pairwise distance by more than one. This recovered all positive distances independently of the author and scorer’s support intersections. ## Geometry, erroneous returns and limits

For arbitrary n, append the negative sum of residual rows. The resulting directed incidence graph has n row vertices, one total vertex and one additional vertex. The n amount edges and the total edge form a spanning tree. Its complementary edges are all the mutable balances. If two ledgers agree on these balances, their differences form a circulation supported on the tree. Flow conservation at a leaf forces its incident edge to carry zero and induction removes the entire tree. Directly, consecutive exact balances determine each amount and hence the total. Reading all mutable balances therefore bounds k by n+1 for an OCR opening or n with a trusted opening. I verified this specific set against every admitted case. For unrestricted balanced ledgers any unqueried cycle supports a nonzero integer circulation. The restricted candidate set can have a smaller instance minimum. One cell suffices exactly when it varies between every distinct pair. Counting cycles alone cannot decide that condition.

For at least two candidate ledgers and at most e adversarially wrong returned cell values, unique correction requires and suffices that d(Q) ≥ 2e+1. At larger distance than 2e, the radius-e Hamming balls are disjoint. At distance at most 2e, split the differing coordinates into two groups of size at most e and construct a word within e substitutions of either candidate. Under this model, reading all mutable cells tolerates one wrong return in **189 of 212 ambiguous grid cases**. In the other 23, even that full read has distance below three. The named counterpart is 9 of 12 ambiguous admitted calls. No external reading was acquired. These guarantees require the true ledger to belong to the supplied candidate set and the selected coordinates to be bound correctly.

A crop covering several cells can have nonadditive cost. Correlated physical errors and faults outside the candidate set require separate evidence. Actual physical accuracy, cost, OCR latency, model performance and posting outcomes remain unmeasured.
