### Why equal bytes can conceal a wrong hook

For zero-based generated input g=618..622, model position t=827+g runs from 1445 to 1449 and predicts x[g+1]=6,1449,16,1328,128822. The 35-gram guard found no complete matching prior 34-token suffix at 2,935 candidate starts. The inactive repetition mask makes C0 and C1 byte-identical for this population. A label claiming “before repeat” attached to an after-repeat read cannot be discriminated by comparing its scores. With a deliberately moving CPU hook, the candidate's real code/frame receipt changes and the scorer reports the cut contradiction. A completely self-consistent substituted receipt remains indistinguishable from its genuine byte-identical evidence at an offline scorer.
