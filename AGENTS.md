## Mathematics in architecture handoffs (eLedgerCare; 2026-11-26 UTC)

Every handoff that proposes or materially changes an algorithm, model, inference rule, or quantitative system guarantee must carry its mathematical argument in the handoff itself. Apply this requirement to successor handoffs and amendments as well as to the original design. Carry forward the full applicable definitions, equations and proofs, or attach their exact version and identify the sections that still apply.

1. State the decision, baseline, proposed change and operating assumptions. Define inputs, outputs, state, indices, dimensions or units, and each symbol where it first appears. Write the baseline and proposed equations and explain their operations in plain language.

2. State each claimed guarantee with its assumptions and scope. Give a checkable derivation or proof, a worked example and a boundary case or counterexample. Mark unresolved obligations explicitly. If the relevant structure is discrete, a state invariant, counting argument or transition proof can supply the model.

3. State each performance hypothesis separately. Name its comparator, evaluation unit, metric, denominator, failure and abstention treatment, proposed decision rule, and an observation that would falsify it. Identify unmeasured quantities and the conditions under which figures generalize.

4. Give the independent reviewer enough information to reconstruct the argument and reproduce the worked checks. Record which claims were proved, observed, disproved or left open. Bind the handoff to its source and evidence revisions. On amendment, identify which premises, proofs and decisions changed.

Apply the depth of the argument to the claim. Routine administrative handoffs may point to the operative design. A mathematical result and a decision to release work each require their own evidence under the existing ownership and review rules.
